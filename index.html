Practicing the Conversion

An accurate historical and materialist analysis requires a grammatical conversion: from the passive voice of "what did not reach us" to the restored agency of "what was actively withheld." This workbook makes the conversion a practice.
From the First Nation-Farmer Climate Unity March project · Jeff Kisling
The passive constructions in which settlers learned this history are not accidents of style — they are the finished product of the withholding, the grammar the silence was designed to produce. Converting them, sentence by sentence, is learnable work. And the conversion is not complete until the resources move.

The Four Moves

Every conversion does the same four things. Click each move to see the question that forces it.

1 · Name an agent

The passive sentence has no subject; the active sentence requires one. "Mistakes were made" is the eternal grammar of the unaccountable.
Who did this, and who carries their name now?

2 · Locate a mechanism

Withholding was implemented — by statute, policy, budget, curriculum, court decision. Mechanisms outlive intentions.
By what instrument was this done, and is the instrument still in operation?

3 · Keep the present tense

"Was taken" sounds finished; "is held" tells the truth. The land is still farmed, the endowment still compounds.
Where is the value now?

4 · Place yourself in the sentence

Not as villain, but as position: member, inheritor, beneficiary, neighbor. Without a speaker, it is still a history lecture.
Where am I standing in this sentence?

The Resources Follow the Sentence

If the history merely "did not reach us," learning it completes the repair and the journey ends in a feeling. But if land, children, language, and truth were actively withheld — taken, with the value banked — then learning is only the audit, and the repair is the return. Five forms. Tap each.

Money

The plainest form, and the most postponed
A recurring gift to Indigenous-led organizations — a budget line that renews without being re-argued. An honor tax paid voluntarily to the nation whose land you live on. A meeting or yearly meeting moving funds toward Indigenous-led work as obligation, not charity. The test is Alvin's question from the Kheprw Institute: what actually changed? A conscience is not a change. A transfer is.

Land

Returned, directed, or opened
Returned outright where possible; placed under Indigenous direction where not; opened without conditions where neither is yet possible. The Decolonial Repair Network's acre in Iowa City is a working example: land acquired to support the Great Plains Action Society, with settlers supplying labor and money under Indigenous leadership — the entire grammar of repair on one small parcel.

Labor

The return available to everyone
Week after week, under Indigenous direction, at the tasks the led community names: work days at the Urban Resilience Hub, Saturday mornings at the mutual aid tables, the wheelbarrow rather than the workshop. Labor carries what money cannot — time, which is the medium every channel is built from.

Institutional weight

A meeting's, a church's, a family's
Minutes that name the taking in the active voice and attach a commitment. Testimony at the Iowa Utilities Board when the next company comes for the next easement. Funding decisions made in consultation with the Native communities concerned. A congregation's platform — pulpit, newsletter, website — handed over so Indigenous voices reach the people those institutions still command.

Story & platform

The lightest resource, the strictest rule
Authority over a story stays with its teller — the lesson Foxy Onefeather taught unforgettably. Amplify under permission; link rather than paraphrase; let the museum-curator instinct die. A website is a return only insofar as it carries people to the sources and leaves the ownership where it was.
The sentence comes first. The resources follow the sentence.
A taking has a taker, a taker has holdings, and holdings can move — the only grammar of repair that the history itself will recognize.
